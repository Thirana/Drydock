<Box kind="problem" title="The problem">

- **Warehouse scanners** need to post stock counts to an inventory API on kade-worker (`10.10.1.20:8443`). They are devices, not people, so IAP sign-in (<Ch n="6">chapter 6</Ch>) does not fit, and the API must not be on the internet.
- **The office accountant** runs nightly reports against Cloud SQL (`10.10.32.3`).
- **Both sides need each other's names**: kade-worker reads files from the office file server `nas.office.kade.lan`, and office PCs should resolve `db.kade.internal` (<Ch n="8">chapter 8</Ch>).
- Today the office reaches GCP only over the public internet, through its router at `198.51.100.20`.

</Box>

## Cloud Router and BGP: who learns which routes

<Recalls><Recall n="12">Routing and BGP</Recall></Recalls>

HA VPN uses **dynamic routing**: the Cloud Router and the office router run BGP over each tunnel, and each tells the other which ranges it can reach (<Ch n="4">chapter 4</Ch>: dynamic routes). Nothing is typed in by hand, and when a tunnel fails its routes disappear by themselves. The important question is **what each side advertises**, because a range that is not advertised simply has no path.

<BgpEx />

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>#</th><th>Kadé advertises</th><th>Why</th><th>Learned from</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>1</td><td>All subnets of kade-vpc (default)</td><td>Office reaches kade-worker and anything else in a subnet</td><td>Automatic</td></tr>
    <tr><td>2</td><td>`10.10.32.0/20`</td><td>Cloud SQL's private services range is **not** a subnet of kade-vpc, so it is not advertised by default</td><td><Ch n="9">Chapter 9</Ch></td></tr>
    <tr><td>3</td><td>`35.199.192.0/19`</td><td>Replies to Cloud DNS forwarding queries (<Ch n="8">chapter 8</Ch>) must find their way back over the VPN</td><td><Ch n="8">Chapter 8</Ch></td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>

<Caption>Adding 2 and 3 means switching the Cloud Router to **custom advertisement**: "all subnets" plus listed ranges (command 6).</Caption>

<Key title="Dynamic routing mode decides how far learned routes reach">

A VPC setting (<Ch n="2">chapter 2</Ch>): in **regional** mode, the office routes the Cloud Router learns are used only by resources in its own region, asia-southeast1. In **global** mode, every region of kade-vpc can use them. Kadé runs in one region, so regional is fine; a second region would need global.

</Key>

## MTU over the tunnel

<Recalls><Recall n="18">MTU, MSS and fragmentation</Recall></Recalls>

IPsec wraps each packet in extra headers before it crosses the internet. The inside packet must therefore be smaller than the outside path allows. <Ch n="1">Chapter 1</Ch> promised this would come back:

<MtuBar />

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>#</th><th>Rule</th><th>Why</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>1</td><td>Cloud VPN carries inner packets up to 1460 bytes</td><td>The same as kade-vpc's default MTU (<Ch n="1">chapter 1</Ch>), so VMs and the tunnel agree</td></tr>
    <tr><td>2</td><td>Set the office side of the tunnel to the same size, and clamp TCP MSS on the office router</td><td>Office PCs think in 1500-byte packets. Clamping MSS (Google's peer guides suggest values around 1360 to 1420) makes TCP pick smaller segments from the start</td></tr>
    <tr><td>3</td><td>If kade-vpc's MTU is ever raised to 1500 or 8896, the VPN does not follow</td><td>Large packets to the office would no longer fit through the tunnel</td></tr>
    <tr><td>4</td><td>The symptom of getting it wrong</td><td>Small things work (ping, DNS, logins); large transfers or big pages hang. The fundamentals course' "black hole" again</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>

## Interconnect, briefly

Interconnect replaces "over the internet" with a private physical path into Google's network. The Cloud Router and BGP part stays exactly as above; the tunnel is replaced by a **VLAN attachment** on the physical link.

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>#</th><th>Consider Interconnect when</th><th>Detail</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>1</td><td>You need more than a few Gbps steadily</td><td>Each VPN tunnel tops out around 3 Gbps</td></tr>
    <tr><td>2</td><td>Latency must be steady</td><td>The internet path a VPN uses changes from hour to hour</td></tr>
    <tr><td>3</td><td>You move a lot of data out of GCP</td><td>Egress over Interconnect is cheaper per GB than internet egress, which can pay for the port</td></tr>
    <tr><td>4</td><td>Your site is near a Google colocation facility, or a partner serves it</td><td>Dedicated needs your equipment in the facility; Partner works through a provider that is already there</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>

<Caption>Interconnect traffic is not encrypted by default. Add MACsec on the link, or run HA VPN over the Interconnect, when encryption is required.</Caption>

<Box kind="cost" title="Cost and limits (checked September 2026)">

HA VPN is billed per tunnel per hour (so two tunnels for HA) plus normal egress for traffic sent from GCP to the office. Interconnect has a monthly port or connection fee plus VLAN attachment charges, with lower egress rates. Check the Cloud VPN and Cloud Interconnect pricing pages for your region.

</Box>

<Box kind="exam" title="Exam angle">

"Encrypted connection over the internet with 99.99% availability" is HA VPN with two tunnels. "10 Gbps or more, private, not over the internet" is Dedicated Interconnect, or Partner Interconnect if no colocation is nearby. "Routes should update automatically when on-premises networks change" means dynamic routing with Cloud Router (BGP). "On-premises cannot reach Cloud SQL's private IP" means the private services range is not advertised, or custom routes are not exported on the peering.

</Box>

## Commands in this chapter

Commands 1 to 4 are read-only. Commands 5 to 7 change the project; the office router is configured separately, with the same secret, addresses and ASNs.

<TryIt n={1} title="Are the tunnels up?">

- **What it shows:** Each tunnel's status and the reason if it is not established.
- **What to look for:** `ESTABLISHED` on both. Other values usually mean a mismatched shared secret or IKE settings.

```sh
gcloud compute vpn-tunnels list --filter="region:asia-southeast1" \
  --format="table(name,status,detailedStatus,peerIp)"
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={2} title="Are the BGP sessions up, and what was learned?">

- **What it shows:** Each BGP peer's state, and the routes learned from the office.
- **What to look for:** Both peers `Established`, and `172.16.0.0/16` in the best routes.

```sh
gcloud compute routers get-status kade-vpn-router --region=asia-southeast1 \
  --format="yaml(result.bgpPeerStatus,result.bestRoutes)"
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={3} title="What does Kadé advertise to the office?">

- **What it shows:** The Cloud Router's advertisement mode, groups and extra ranges.
- **What to look for:** `CUSTOM`, `ALL_SUBNETS`, and both extra ranges.

```sh
gcloud compute routers describe kade-vpn-router --region=asia-southeast1 \
  --format="yaml(bgp)"
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={4} title="Test a full-size packet to the office">

- **What it shows:** From kade-worker, whether 1460-byte packets cross the tunnel without fragmenting.
- **What to look for:** Replies at 1432 bytes of data. If small pings work and this one fails, it is MTU.

```sh
ping -c 3 -M do -s 1432 172.16.1.40
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={5} title="Create the gateways (changes the project)">

- **What it shows:** The HA VPN gateway, and a resource describing the office router.
- **What to look for:** The two public IPs GCP assigns to kade-havpn, which go into the office router's config.

```sh
gcloud compute vpn-gateways create kade-havpn --network=kade-vpc --region=asia-southeast1
gcloud compute external-vpn-gateways create kade-office-gw --interfaces=0=198.51.100.20
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={6} title="Create the router and one tunnel with its BGP session (changes the project)">

- **What it shows:** The Cloud Router, tunnel 0, and its BGP session. Repeat the last three commands for interface 1 and 169.254.1.0/30.
- **What to look for:** Commands 1 and 2 report the tunnel and session up once the office side is configured.

```sh
gcloud compute routers create kade-vpn-router --network=kade-vpc \
  --region=asia-southeast1 --asn=64512
gcloud compute vpn-tunnels create kade-tunnel-0 --region=asia-southeast1 \
  --vpn-gateway=kade-havpn --interface=0 \
  --peer-external-gateway=kade-office-gw --peer-external-gateway-interface=0 \
  --ike-version=2 --shared-secret=REPLACE-WITH-A-LONG-SECRET --router=kade-vpn-router
gcloud compute routers add-interface kade-vpn-router --region=asia-southeast1 \
  --interface-name=if-tunnel-0 --vpn-tunnel=kade-tunnel-0 --ip-address=169.254.0.1 --mask-length=30
gcloud compute routers add-bgp-peer kade-vpn-router --region=asia-southeast1 \
  --peer-name=office-0 --interface=if-tunnel-0 \
  --peer-ip-address=169.254.0.2 --peer-asn=65010
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={7} title="Advertise the extra ranges (changes the project)">

- **What it shows:** Switches to custom advertisement: all subnets, plus Cloud SQL's range and Cloud DNS's forwarding range.
- **What to look for:** The office router's BGP table now lists both ranges via the tunnels.

```sh
gcloud compute routers update kade-vpn-router --region=asia-southeast1 \
  --advertisement-mode=CUSTOM --set-advertisement-groups=ALL_SUBNETS \
  --set-advertisement-ranges=10.10.32.0/20,35.199.192.0/19
```

</TryIt>
