## The few metrics that matter

Logs are records of events; **metrics** are numbers sampled over time, cheap to keep and fast to chart. GCP collects most of them automatically, at no charge for Google's own services. For a web platform, four "golden signals" measured at the front door cover most of what users feel:

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>#</th><th>Golden signal</th><th>Question</th><th>Metric (Cloud Monitoring)</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>1</td><td>**Traffic**</td><td>How busy are we?</td><td>`loadbalancing.googleapis.com/https/request_count`</td></tr>
    <tr><td>2</td><td>**Errors**</td><td>What share of requests fail?</td><td>The same, split by `response_code_class` (5xx divided by all)</td></tr>
    <tr><td>3</td><td>**Latency**</td><td>How slow are the slow requests?</td><td>`https/total_latencies`, at the 95th and 99th percentile, not the average</td></tr>
    <tr><td>4</td><td>**Saturation**</td><td>How close to a limit are we?</td><td>Cloud Run `container/instance_count` against max instances, Cloud SQL connections, NAT ports</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>

<Caption>Measure at the load balancer, because that is what shoppers experience. The backend's own numbers miss requests that never reached it.</Caption>

## Uptime checks: is the shop reachable from outside?

An uptime check calls a URL from several places around the world every minute or few, and records whether the right answer came back. It is the closest thing to a shopper trying the site, and it works even when no shopper is online. It can also warn before the certificate expires.

## Alerts that people do not learn to ignore

An alert that fires for nothing teaches you to ignore alerts. Three habits keep them meaningful:

<Table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>#</th><th>Habit</th><th>Why</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>1</td><td>**Alert on symptoms users feel**</td><td>"5% of requests fail" is something to act on. "CPU at 80%" often is not</td></tr>
    <tr><td>2</td><td>**Use ratios, not counts**</td><td>50 errors in a minute is a disaster at 100 requests and nothing at 100,000. The error **ratio** stays meaningful as traffic grows</td></tr>
    <tr><td>3</td><td>**Require a duration**</td><td>"Above the threshold for 5 minutes" ignores single spikes. The chart below shows the difference</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</Table>

<Caption>"Phone" alerts mean shoppers are affected now. "Email / chat" alerts mean something needs attention today. A budget alert on the billing account belongs in the second group too.</Caption>

<Key title="A step up: SLOs">

A service level objective says "99.5% of requests succeed, measured over 30 days". Cloud Monitoring can track one and alert on its **burn rate**: how fast you are using up the 0.5% allowance. A short burst of errors burns slowly and waits; a serious outage burns fast and alerts immediately. It replaces alerts 2 and 3 with something calmer, once the basics work.

</Key>

## Commands in this chapter

Commands 1 to 3 are read-only. Commands 4 to 6 change the project.

<TryIt n={1} title="Find the IP ranges uptime checks come from">

- **What it shows:** The source addresses of Google's uptime checkers, by region.
- **What to look for:** Only needed if you check the origin directly and must allow them (section 03).

```sh
gcloud monitoring uptime list-ips
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={2} title="Run a Connectivity Test and read the result">

- **What it shows:** Whether kade-worker can reach Cloud SQL on 5432, and the step that decides it.
- **What to look for:** `REACHABLE`, or the step where it says `DROPPED` and why.

```sh
gcloud network-management connectivity-tests create worker-to-sql \
  --source-instance=projects/kade-prod/zones/asia-southeast1-a/instances/kade-worker \
  --destination-ip-address=10.10.32.3 --destination-port=5432 --protocol=TCP
gcloud network-management connectivity-tests describe worker-to-sql \
  --format="yaml(reachabilityDetails.result,reachabilityDetails.traces[0].steps)"
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={3} title="List alert policies">

- **What it shows:** Every alert policy and whether it is enabled.
- **What to look for:** The eight from section 04, all enabled, each with a notification channel.

```sh
gcloud alpha monitoring policies list --format="table(displayName,enabled,notificationChannels.len())"
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={4} title="Create an uptime check (changes the project)">

- **What it shows:** An HTTPS check of the health URL, every minute, from three regions, expecting a 200.
- **What to look for:** Results in the console under Monitoring → Uptime checks within a few minutes.

```sh
gcloud monitoring uptime create kade-api-health \
  --resource-type=uptime-url --resource-labels=host=api.kade.lk,project_id=kade-prod \
  --protocol=https --path=/v1/health --period=1 \
  --regions=asia-pacific,europe,usa-oregon --status-codes=200
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={5} title="Count Cloud Armor denies as a metric (changes the project)">

- **What it shows:** A log-based metric that counts denied requests, which alert 8 can watch.
- **What to look for:** The metric `logging.googleapis.com/user/kade_armor_denies` in Metrics Explorer after some traffic.

```sh
gcloud logging metrics create kade_armor_denies \
  --description="Requests denied by Cloud Armor" \
  --log-filter='resource.type="http_load_balancer" AND jsonPayload.enforcedSecurityPolicy.outcome="DENY"'
```

</TryIt>

<TryIt n={6} title="Create a notification channel (changes the project)">

- **What it shows:** An email channel for "email / chat" alerts. Phone and chat apps are added the same way, or in the console.
- **What to look for:** The channel's ID in the output, used when creating alert policies.

```sh
gcloud beta monitoring channels create --display-name="Kadé platform email" \
  --type=email --channel-labels=email_address=platform@kade.lk
```

</TryIt>
